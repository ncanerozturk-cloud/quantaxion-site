The difference

Screeners rank. QuantAxion reasons.

A filter finds companies matching a number. It cannot tell you that a memory supplier being sold out through 2027 is the reason a networking designer's margins hold, or that an insider sale lands in the same window as a policy change. Those are connections across sources, made over time — and connecting them is the work. That is the engine we built.

A screener returns

  • A composite score, with no way to interrogate it
  • “Sentiment: positive” — detached from the story behind it
  • “Congress is buying” — without who, how much, or how stale
  • Fundamentals and technicals in separate, unlinked tabs
  • The same confident tone regardless of what it actually read

QuantAxion returns

  • A conditional call with the price levels it applies at
  • The causal mechanism, stated in plain language
  • Fundamentals, positioning and price structure in one argument
  • Every claim bound to a dated filing, article or print
  • The strongest case against its own view, and how far coverage reaches
One thesis, three layers

Most tools pick one. A decision needs all three.

Fundamentals tell you what a business earns. Technicals tell you what price the market is asking. Neither tells you what is about to change — that comes from everything being said and disclosed around the company. QuantAxion reads all three and resolves them against each other in a single argument.

The engine

Read each source once. Connect it everywhere.

The expensive part of research is reading, and the valuable part is what a source implies about companies it never names. Each source is stored as an immutable, dated version and read once into observations that are reused across themes, suppliers and customers — so a memory-pricing disclosure reaches a networking designer's margin outlook without being re-read, and a new event updates the earlier conclusion instead of replacing it.

01

Collect

Filings, news, policy and insider disclosures, Fed language, macro series, prices and option chains — each stored with the date it was published and the date it was fetched.

02

Version

Sources are immutable. A revised article becomes a new version beside the old one, so a conclusion can always be traced to the text that produced it.

03

Extract once

Dated observations are pulled under a broad contract and reused across themes and companies, instead of re-reading the archive per ticker.

04

Reason

Claude Opus assembles the mechanism: what documented exposure links the event to this company's economics, and what competing explanation would fit the same facts.

05

Check

Figures are re-derived, price geometry is validated, and every quoted claim must bind to a stored source or it does not ship.

◎

Conditional, never predictive

Scenarios are stated as conditions with the levels that trigger them, and labelled as the system's own reasoning rather than analyst consensus. A level is where an argument becomes actionable — not a price target dressed up as one.

⚑

Four states, never flattened

Missing, stale, unverified and measured zero stay distinct through the whole pipeline. Most tools collapse them into one reassuring sentence, which is precisely where a thesis quietly stops being supported.

⌘

Precision is never invented

Congressional amounts are disclosed as ranges and stay ranges. A projected earnings date is labelled a projection. Where an article was only available as a summary, the report says so rather than citing it as a full read.

▲

Research, not execution

No brokerage connection and no orders. The separation is also analytical: a constructive business case and a specific option trade are two different decisions, and the reports keep them apart.
