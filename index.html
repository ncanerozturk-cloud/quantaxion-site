The problem

A score tells you nothing you can argue with.

Most tools rank stocks by a composite number. You cannot check a number, you cannot disagree with it, and it cannot tell you that it was working from a headline instead of the filing. The useful question is never “what is the score” — it is “what is happening, how do you know, and what would change your mind.”

What a screener gives you

  • A composite score of 73 — derived how?
  • “Sentiment: positive” — because of what story?
  • “Congress is buying” — who, how much, how stale?
  • Confident prose whether or not the data exists
  • Silence about what it could not read

What QuantAxion gives you

  • A conditional verdict with the price it applies at
  • The causal mechanism, in plain language
  • Every claim traced to a dated filing, article or print
  • The strongest argument against the view
  • An explicit list of what it could not read

How it works

Read each source once. Reuse what it said everywhere.

The expensive part of research is reading. QuantAxion stores every source as an immutable, dated version and extracts observations from it once, so the same filing informs a semiconductor theme, a supplier’s outlook and a specific company view without being re-read — and a new event updates the earlier conclusion rather than replacing it.

01

Collect

Filings, news, policy and insider disclosures, Fed language, macro series, prices and option chains — each stored with the date it was published and the date it was fetched.

02

Version

Sources are immutable. A revised article becomes a new version beside the old one, so a conclusion can always be traced to the text that produced it.

03

Extract once

Dated observations are pulled under a broad contract and reused across themes and companies, instead of re-reading the archive per ticker.

04

Reason

Claude Opus assembles the mechanism: what documented exposure links the event to this company's economics, and what competing explanation would fit the same facts.

05

Check

Figures are re-derived, price geometry is validated, and every quoted claim must bind to a stored source or it does not ship.

Limits

What it will not do.

A research tool that hides its gaps is worse than one that has none, because you cannot tell which claims to trust. These are stated on every report.

◎

It does not predict prices

Scenarios are conditional and labelled as the system’s own reasoning — never presented as analyst consensus or a forecast. A level is where an argument becomes actionable, not a target.

⚑

It separates missing from zero

“No filings found”, “collection failed”, “stale” and “measured zero” are four different states and are never collapsed into one reassuring sentence.

⌘

It does not invent precision

Congressional amounts are disclosed as ranges and stay ranges. An estimated earnings date is labelled an estimate. Full-text coverage is reported honestly where articles were truncated to summaries.

▲

It does not execute

No brokerage connection, no orders, no portfolio management. It is decision support, and a bullish business case is explicitly not the same decision as buying a specific option.
